Thesis

Enhancing Emergency Preparedness through Immersive Virtual Reality: A Comparative Study of Stress Responses in Radiological Simulations

Immersive virtual reality (IVR) shows promise for hazardous‐materials training but its ability to elicit realistic stress has been unclear. In this study, 55 professional firefighters were randomly assigned to either an IVR or a real‐world (RW) radiological‐incident simulation. Using pre‑/post‑surveys, a scenario task, and a new Radiation Source Stress Questionnaire, I found that while behavioral and cognitive reactions were virtually identical in both groups, those in IVR reported significantly greater emotional concern and perceived risk. Regression analyses also revealed that individuals with higher risk‑taking propensities experienced less stress overall, and that IVR sessions ran slightly longer, though duration effects were deemed negligible. These results suggest IVR can match—or even heighten—certain stress responses seen in real life, offering potential benefits for memory retention in training but also raising caution about over‑amplified risk perceptions. Further longitudinal work with physiological measures is recommended to refine how simulated stress should be calibrated in emergency‐responder education.

MVE-VR

An all-inclusive training solution for vehicle inspections made for the Iowa Department of Transportation that I served as a developer on.

With MVE-VR, put your trainees through a level VI vehicle inspection and immediately conduct a segmented After Action Review. This review breaks down every portion of the vehicle inspection and allows you and your trainee to view an interactive simulation playback from different camera angles of what the trainee saw whilst in Virtual Reality.

In this full scale vehicle inspection simulator trainees can:

  • Interact with the driver and review their documents
  • Check lights and vehicle functions
  • Gather radiation readings of shipments
  • Inspect items and view their statuses
  • Go inside the cab and inspect instruments
  • Get under the hood and view critical parts
  • Take pictures and audio recordings of your findings

Before and after an inspection Instructors are able to:

  • Author their own vehicle damage generator for a more direct inspection
  • Put trainees in a completely randomized or a previously authored inspection
  • Spectate an inspection that is being conducted on another computer
  • Add users that need to conduct inspections
  • Review past inspections with a detailed segmented After Action Review

I personally developed:

  • Many of the inspection elements and grabbables
    • Brakes and other undercarriage interactables
    • Lug nuts and tires
    • Cabin including wheel and accurate reading pressure gauges
    • Document system
    • Lights
  • Inspection System
    • Tooltips that pop up when physically interacting with elements
    • Audio recording that can be saved and viewed later
    • Picture taking that can be saved and viewed later
    • This system has been used in other projects
